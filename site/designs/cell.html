What a cell is

A cell is a small team of AI agents with one job. It runs in your environment, meaning your own accounts and repositories, with a conductor agent that hands out work and keeps the record, and keys that you hold to steer it and take it over.

The first offer is the website cell: an agent team builds a public static website from your photos, videos and description, and hands it over in a GitHub repository you own.

What it sells is “the setup, the guidance and the trust”; the software itself is free and open. The name is always plural, and the “CC” stands for Creative Commons.

Status, as the company states it: “No cell of any size has been sold.” Every larger price is labelled a first proposal.

Sources: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 18

Who it is for

  • “Teams that want AI agents doing real work, inside their own accounts and repositories, and want to see and stop what those agents do.”
  • For the website cell: artists, musicians, AI people and technologists, consultants and companies.
  • Partners: freelancers, small consultancies and larger firms who bring customers.
  • Not for anyone who wants a hosted service that keeps their data.

Sources: 1, 2, 12

Sizes and offers

  • Website cell ↗

    A static site from your photos, videos and description, in your own GitHub. The page shows a £250 launch price, usually £500, which it says may change; nothing has been sold yet.

  • Cell sizes ↗

    One agent, a few sessions, a team, or a network of conductors: £500, £1,500, £5,000 and £20,000, all labelled first proposal.

  • The console ↗

    A read-only view of the board, agents, keys and activity, built for its own team.

  • The hub ↗

    Lists every cell made: “We list; we do not sell.”

  • Partner programme ↗

    Freelancers, consultancies and firms sell; the sale is split 50/50. No partner has signed yet.

Sources: 2, 6, 12, 16, 17
